## 1. The goal, stated correctly

"Win the first two rounds" = **advance out of the December qualifier, then podium at the
CA Southern Championship in January.**

Advancement is **not** the robot-game score. It's the **Champion's Award**, a blended
ranking across four equally weighted areas (25% each):

| Area | Scored by | How |
|---|---|---|
| Robot Game | Referees (objective) | Best of 3 × 2.5-min matches |
| Robot Design | Judges (rubric) | 5-min explanation + Q&A |
| Innovation Project | Judges (rubric) | 5-min live presentation + Q&A |
| Core Values | Judges + referees (rubric) | Observed across judging session + robot game |

**Strategic consequence:** aim to be top-quartile in *all four*, not #1 in one and
mid-pack elsewhere. A team with a reliable ~200 robot, a real project, a clear design
story, and strong Core Values beats a 340-robot team that is thin on the judged areas —
almost every year.

There is a separate **Robot Performance Award** for the top robot score. It is usually
*not* an advancement slot. Do not build the season around it.

## 3. Meeting time — the binding constraint

2 hrs/week × 14 weeks ≈ **28 hrs/team**. A team that advances typically invests
**60–100 hrs**. Current budget is ~1/3 of that, and the robot game compresses worst.

**Recommendation to school / parents:**

| | Ask |
|---|---|
| Core weekly | **2 sessions, ~4 hrs/week total** (e.g. one weeknight 2 hr + one weekend 2–3 hr) |
| November ramp | **5–6 hrs/week** for the 3 weeks before the qualifier |
| Home tasks | ~1 hr/week — presentation scripts, project research, attachment sketches |
| Mock tournament | One ~4-hr Saturday in early December, all three teams |

That lands ~65–75 hrs/team — competitive without burning out 9–14-year-olds.

- **Two short sessions beat one long block.** Focus drops after ~2 hrs at this age, and
  iteration needs a gap between sessions (test → sleep on it → fix).
- **Bigger lever than raw hours: one assistant coach / parent mentor per team.** 3 teams
  solo is not sustainable, and adult-run robots are penalized by judges. Pitch to parents
  now: "2 hrs/week at the table, no robotics experience needed."
- Pitch the increase as concrete asks against a calendar, not "we need more time."

**If more time is truly impossible:** stay at 2 hrs/week but narrow hard — 3 missions max
per team + the 70 free points (§5), lean into the judged areas (they move forward with
home work), and let one team go all-in while the other two aim to participate well.

## 4. Three teams, mixed motivation

Some kids want to compete; some want to participate. That's fine — the corrosive thing is
a **time-commitment mismatch inside one team**. Solve it by composition, **before rosters
lock**.

**Sort the three teams by commitment:**

| Team | Goal | Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| "Going for it" | Advance from the qualifier | Full plan: 2 sessions/wk + Nov ramp + home tasks; kid **and** parent sign off up front |
| "Strong participation" ×2 | Do all 4 areas well, learn, have fun, still compete & present | Core weekly session; extras optional |

Both are legitimate FLL seasons. Frame it as *different goals*, not "A team / B team."

- **Survey every family this week:** realistic weekly hours, weekend availability,
  willingness to do home tasks, and — chase advancement or enjoy the season?
- **Guided self-selection:** kids/parents pick; coach places borderline cases; keep
  friend groups together.
- Put your **strongest assistant coach on the participation teams**, not just the
  competitive one.
- Keep it humane: own team names/identities, no stigma language; doors stay open (a
  participation kid who catches fire can sub at the tournament or move up next year); a
  kid who's overwhelmed can step back without shame.
- **Protect fun on every team, including the competitive one** — "we celebrate what we
  do" is a scored Core Value, and un-fun competitive teams lose kids mid-season.
- Across teams: same role names everywhere so kids can sub; occasional joint huddles
  (all Strategists together, all Coders together) — that cross-help is Coopertition
  evidence.

## 5. Robot game strategy

### Bank the free points first (~70/match, every robot)

- **Equipment inspection — 20:** all kit (including the M13 team-built keystone model)
  fits in one launch area, under 305 mm tall.
- **Precision Tokens — 50:** start with 6; each interruption outside Home costs one.
  Drill launch-and-return so the team never touches the robot outside Home.

Most rookie teams bleed 15–35 points here. Getting this right is worth more than any
single mission and it's pure practice, not engineering.

### Pick missions by points ÷ (time + risk)

Strong early candidates (verify point values against the missions video):

| Mission | Pts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **M13 Keystone Species** | 30 | The model is *your* build → you control its geometry → low variance |
| **M08 Tangled** | 30 | Single condition (vine touches mat); shares a region with M09 |
| **M09 Research Platform** | 30 | Same region as M08 — one attachment run can chase both |
| **M12 Forest Elder** | 30 | High base, decisive action |
| **M03 Flip the Rock** | 20 + 10 | Single decisive action |
| **M01 Drone Survey** | 20 + 10 | Single decisive action |
| **M15 Biocentric Architecture** | 30 + 10 | Only if one feature is reliably reachable; then park it on the matching dock (see [strategy.md](strategy.md)) |

**Defer / skip:** M02, M06, M14 (per-object counting → variance killers); M07's
cross-table +10 (needs an opposing team — unavailable in practice, low value live).

### Reliability rule

A mission enters the competition run **only after 8/10 successful practice attempts.**
A repeatable 220 beats a one-time 340 that craters to 120 twice — and judges watch all
three matches, so consistency also feeds the Robot Design score.

**Target: a repeatable ~180–230 per team for Round 1.** Fill the real number into
[missions/README.md](missions/README.md) once object counts are verified.

## 6. The 14-week plan (Sept 8 → Dec 13 qualifier)

Weekly rhythm each 2-hr session: 10 min Core Values check-in / build-log update ·
40 min Robot Game (rotate table time across teams) · 40 min Innovation Project ·
20 min reflection + assign next session · 10 min cleanup.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Sep 8 – Sep 28)

| Track | Done when |
|---|---|
| Robot Game | All 15 mission models built; field set up; **equipment inspection passing** on all 3 robots |
| Robot base | Gyro drive-straight + turn + square-to-wall reliable on all 3 robots ([programs/templates](../../programs/templates/)) |
| Launch discipline | Launch + return with zero robot contact outside Home |
| Innovation Project | **Problem locked per team** — local + narrow (a specific SoCal habitat / species / corridor tied to a Project Spark). Expert emails sent (3–4 each) |
| Core Values | Team names; roles assigned & rotating; **build log started** (photos + dates) |

### Phase 2 — Core build (Sep 29 – Oct 26)

| Track | Done when |
|---|---|
| Robot Game | Each team locks a **4–6 mission set**; repeatable ~150–200 |
| Attachments | Fast, tool-free swap system; each attachment does one job |
| Innovation Project | **Expert interview done**; prototype (model or drawing) built; first outside feedback logged |
| Core Values | Every kid can explain some part of their robot |

### Phase 3 — Consistency + iteration (Oct 27 – Nov 23)

| Track | Done when |
|---|---|
| Robot Game | **8/10 rule** applied — cut what doesn't pass; full timed 2.5-min matches, scored |
| Innovation Project | 2nd iteration from feedback ("changed X because Y"); 5-min presentation drafted, every member speaks |
| Robot Design | 5-min explanation written to hit every rubric row; Q&A rehearsed with a stranger |
| Core Values | GP at the table rehearsed — greet/thank refs, composure on a failed run |

### Phase 4 — Polish + mock tournament (Nov 24 – Dec 12)

- **Full mock tournament ~Dec 6** — outside adult runs a real judging session + 3 timed
  matches per team. Fix what breaks.
- Final week: backup attachments, packing lists, rest. **No new missions after Dec 6.**

Rubrics unchanged; the bar rises to "excellent." Line this up *before* the qualifier:

  contact) and report what happened. "We presented to X and they're considering it" wins.
- **Presentations:** every rubric row, every member speaks, no cards.
- Keep the described robot aligned with what the team actually ran — judges cross-check.

## 8. Competition-day roles (assign ~2 weeks out)

- **Robot game:** exactly **2 handlers at the table**, a **different pair each of the 3
  matches** (6 kids get a turn) + 1 on attachment/queue prep. Rehearse the handoffs.
- **Judging:** script so **every kid delivers a real segment**. Give a shy kid a short
  rehearsed part they own; keep them out of the open Q&A crossfire. Participation balance
  is scored.
